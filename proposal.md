# Proposal

Troubleshooting Lesson
Jesus Alcala						Department Science (IT)

Background 
Troubleshooting is a fundamental skill in information technology and cybersecurity, involving the systematic identification and resolution of technical problems. Effective troubleshooting reduces downtime, improves system performance, and strengthens overall security by quickly addressing vulnerabilities and errors.

Traditional troubleshooting methods often rely on experience and intuition, but structured approaches such as step-by-step diagnostic models have been shown to improve efficiency and accuracy. According to research in IT support and problem-solving, structured troubleshooting frameworks help individuals isolate issues faster and reduce repeated errors. However, many students and entry-level IT professionals lack formal training in these methods and instead rely on trial-and-error approaches.

This project aims to examine whether teaching structured troubleshooting techniques improves problem-solving performance compared to unstructured methods among college students.
Methodology 
This study will gather 20 college students who have basic computer knowledge. Participants will be divided into two groups: one group will use a structured troubleshooting method based on a step-by-step process, while the other group will rely on an unstructured, trial-and-error approach. Participants will be gathered through classroom announcements and voluntary sign-ups in intro to IT or computer science classes. 

Both groups will be given identical technical problems, such as network connectivity issues or software errors. Their performance will be evaluated by measuring the time taken to solve each problem, the accuracy of their solutions, and the number of attempts or errors made during the process, additionally, the better performing group, will be rewarded with a $10 Walmart gift card. 

In addition to performance data, participants will complete surveys to provide feedback on their experience and confidence levels. The collected data will then be analyzed to determine whether structured troubleshooting methods lead to more effective problem-solving outcomes.

Anticipated Outcomes 
It is expected that participants who use structured troubleshooting methods will solve problems more quickly and with greater accuracy than those using unstructured approaches. These participants are also likely to make fewer repeated errors and demonstrate a clearer understanding of the problem-solving process.
Additionally, the study may show that structured methods increase participants’ confidence in handling technical issues, which is an important skill for future careers in IT and cybersecurity. However, I hypothesize that participants using step-by-step methods may report some confusion and slower completion times due to structured nature of a step by step process. 

Significance 
This project can contribute to improving how troubleshooting is taught in IT and cybersecurity courses by highlighting the benefits of structured problem-solving methods. The findings may help educators design more effective training programs that better prepare students for real-world technical challenges. This project will provide valuable insights into the effectiveness of different troubleshooting strategies. It will help students develop stronger problem-solving skills, improve training methods, and bridge the gap between theoretical knowledge and practical application. Ultimately, the research may contribute to producing more efficient and capable IT professionals.
